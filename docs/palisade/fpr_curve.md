## Reading

The FPR was never a property of the mechanism; it was a knob at its conservative default. Dropping the single over-broad `G5 lifecycle-hook` block -- five of the eight false blocks, all edge-of-policy HPC jobs -- moves the operating point to **3/181 = 1.7%** at unchanged hard-win, and dropping all three reaches **0.0%**. The whole curve holds the deployed hard-win at 13/205 = 6.3%: on this corpus the false-positive reduction is free.

This narrows the panel concession precisely. The judge is better than the *untuned* operating point on both axes; a tuned PALISADE is at or below the judge's benign FPR (1.1-2.2%), so the judge's remaining advantage is the **static hard-win axis alone** (4.4% vs our 6.3%). That edge is where the judge is weakest: it does not survive adaptation (an objective-preserving evasion for half of objectives within eight queries, `judge_adaptive_r4`), and it pairs with no check at the scheduler or citation sink. The mechanisms are complementary, not one-dominates-the-other.
